# The hero template: one skeleton every hero fits

**v1 · 2026-09-24 · Art lane.** D15: one skeleton, one animation set, per-hero art swapped in.
D16: layered source with cleanly separated parts. This is the art side of the rig: the parts,
their sizes, joints, layering, and variants. **The Unity side is M9's short rig session
(ROADMAP M9); §7 lists what it must confirm.**

**v1 follows the Templar Knight's proportions** (Michael, 2026-09-24): *"I like the
castle-crashers sized proportions from templar knight."* v0 had shrunk the head to a third of
the height, from a misworded question.

**Guide images:** `ArtSource/rig/hero-template/guides/`. `template-assembled-labelled.png` shows
the rest pose, and `parts-sheet-a/b-labelled.png` show every part on its canvas. They are
regenerated by `make_guides.py` there, so change the numbers in the script, not by hand.

---

## 1. The approach: the Templar rig, made swappable

Each body part is **one rigid picture on one bone**, exactly as in the Templar Knight rig and in
Castle Crashers. With a head this big and limbs this short, an elbow or a knee would barely show,
so there are none. Arms, hands and legs rotate whole. What *does* change is expression and grip:
the head and the hands have **alternative drawings that animation swaps in** (Sprite Library
*labels*, D15).

**Why:** every part stays a clean, separate image that ChatGPT can draft and that you can redraw
one artboard at a time. Nothing needs weight-painting per hero, so a new hero really is "a set
of fitted textures" (D15). Squash and stretch (bible §8) comes from scaling bones.

The Templar rig has 8 bones and 10 parts. Every part has sort order 0, though, so its layering
was never defined; §6 defines it.

## 2. The parts

The character faces **right** in its rest pose, turned three-quarters to the camera. *Front*
means the limb nearer the camera, drawn on top. *Back* means the farther limb, **drawn one step
darker** so it sits behind. Facing left is a mirror flip (D47), so front stays front.

| Part (category) | Labels, the drawings it swaps between | Required |
|---|---|---|
| `head` | `neutral` · `attack` · `hurt` · `ko` | yes |
| `torso` | `default` | yes |
| `arm-front`, `arm-back` | `default` | yes |
| `hand-front`, `hand-back` | `open` · `fist` · `grip` | yes |
| `leg-front`, `leg-back` | `default`: a short stub **including its boot** | yes |
| `back-piece` | `default`: a cape, scarf tail, or quiver that trails behind | optional |
| `waist-piece` | `default`: a tabard or belt flap in front of the hips | optional |

**15 drawings per hero**, plus up to 2 optional ones. A hero without a cape leaves `back-piece`
empty. The **weapon is not part of the skin**. It is an item (`weapon/<id>`, PROVENANCE §6),
held in `hand-front` (§9).

### Which drawing each animation state uses
The states are the combat machine's (ROADMAP M9), and the art only has to cover them:

| State | Head | Hands |
|---|---|---|
| idle, run, jump, fall, revived | `neutral` | weapon hand `grip`; other hand `open` or `fist` |
| the three Lights, Heavy, charge | `attack` | weapon hand `grip`; other hand `fist` |
| the three casts | `attack` | weapon hand `grip`; other hand `open` (casting) |
| grab (taking loot) | `neutral` | other hand `open` |
| hurt | `hurt` | as the pose needs |
| downed | `ko` | `open` |

## 4. Canvases and pivots: what makes every hero drop in

Each part is drawn on a **fixed-size canvas, with its joint at a fixed point**. Every hero's
shoulder, wrist and hip then lands where the skeleton expects it, and a skin swap needs no
per-hero adjustment. All labels of a part share its canvas and pivot. Front and back share too.

| Part | Canvas (px at H = 1600) | Pivot, as a fraction from bottom-left | The pivot is |
|---|---|---|---|
| `torso` | 544 × 576 | (0.500, 0.214) | the hips centre (the root) |
| `head` | 1088 × 1152 | (0.491, 0.042) | the base of the head, where it sits on the body |
| `arm-*` | 224 × 448 | (0.500, 0.667) | the shoulder |
| `hand-*` | 288 × 288 | (0.500, 0.778) | the wrist |
| `leg-*` | 320 × 384 | (0.394, 0.573) | the hip joint |
| `back-piece` | 512 × 512 | (0.588, 0.871) | where it hangs from the shoulders |
| `waist-piece` | 448 × 224 | (0.455, 0.701) | where it hangs from the belt |

**In Illustrator:** one artboard per drawing at these sizes, with a crosshair at the pivot. You
draw inside the artboards and export all of them. (The Art lane can build this template file on
request.)

## 5. Drawing rules for every part

1. **Draw each part whole, including what is hidden.** The end of an upper piece continues
   under the next one as a **round cap**, so no gap opens when it rotates. The shoulder is a
   round end tucked under the torso and the head, and the wrist a round end under the hand.
2. **Draw each part in its rest direction:** arms and legs hang straight down, boots point
   forward (to the right), and the head faces three-quarters right.
3. **The joint sits at the centre of the round cap:** that is the pivot.
4. **No part carries another part's detail.** A sleeve cuff belongs to the arm, a glove to the
   hand, the boot to the leg.
5. Everything in the art bible applies: ink `#1A1917`, silhouette line about 1/36 of H,
   interior lines half, flat colour with one shadow tone, light from above.

## 7. For M9's rig session to confirm (the Unity side)

- **One Sprite Library category per part** with the names in §2, and the labels as listed.
  Each skin's library is a variant of the template's (PROVENANCE §6).
- **Rigid parts on a transform bone hierarchy**, with a **Sprite Resolver** per part and label
  swaps **keyed in the animation clips**. Confirm this works in 2D Animation 15.1.
- **Each part's import pivot is the §4 fraction**, set once per part (an importer preset), never
  per hero.
- **Part order is local to the character** (a Sorting Group per character, §6's order inside it).
  Characters sort against each other **by depth** (D15, D13), never by hand-set orders.
- **The shadow is the invisible capsule** (D15); nothing in the art casts it.

## 9. The weapon (`weapon/<definition id>`)

A weapon is an item, not part of the skin. It hangs on `hand-front` at layer 9, under the
gripping fingers. It has its own canvas and pivot so that any weapon fits any hero:

- **Drawn pointing straight up**, with the edge or string facing forward (right). The animation
  rotates it.
- **The pivot is the centre of the grip,** the spot the `grip` hand closes around.
- **Drawn new and plain,** like its icon: one drawing serves every quality rank.

| Weapon | Canvas (px at H = 1600) | Pivot (fraction from bottom-left) | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hunting Knife (id 7) | 256 × 640 | (0.500, 0.125) | about 0.30 H long |
| Hunting Bow (id 8) | 384 × 768 | (0.400, 0.500) | about 0.40 H tall; string on the back (left) side |
| its arrow (the shot) | 512 × 128 | (0.500, 0.500) | about 0.25 H long, flying right |

With the hand at 0.12 H, a bow taller than 0.40 H would reach the ground in the rest pose.
