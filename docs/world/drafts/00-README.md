# Story drafts — five versions for Michael and Marco to choose from

Written 2026-09-26 at Michael's request, with D56's "Claude invents no lore" rule relaxed for
this task by Michael. His brief: keep it medieval fantasy like Castle Crashers; magic is allowed.

Every draft starts from the same prelude (`../PRELUDE_NOTES.md`) and the same Q1 answer
(`../SESSION_NOTES.md`): the four knights are low-level members of the Knight Squad, they go out on
their ordinary duty, pass the crashed ship, learn the aliens want an artifact and that the aliens
amp up ordinary monsters, and venture out to find out why. Somewhere along the way they find the
king badly hurt.

What the drafts change is everything that is still open:

All five stay in the fantasy land. Drafts 1 and 3 end with one fight aboard the alien's ship,
which the prelude already puts in the courtyard; drafts 2, 4 and 5 never touch it. Draft 5 is
the most purely medieval: the land has no word for "alien" and treats it as a star-beast.

Every draft keeps to what the game can show: no dialogue, no cutscenes, beats are things that
happen on stage while the player keeps control (D56 as amended). Each chapter ends with a
**handoff**, the thing on screen that tells even a player who skipped the crawl where to go next.
Each draft ends with a "speeding through" summary: the story as a player who reads nothing would
understand it.

Hero names are deliberately left blank. The four knights are referred to by their element.
Kingdom, king, alien and artifact names are working names, swappable.

## Assumptions made while drafting

- The full story is four chapters plus a finale (draft 4 has five, one per element). Early Access
  ships the prelude and Chapters 1 and 2 (ROADMAP), so those two are the most detailed in every
  draft, and every draft's Chapter 2 ends on something a demo can stop on.
- The knights are Knight Squad members, the lowest rank. The tutorial is played as the chosen
  hero or heroes.
- The mutated slimes' colours (red, purple, blue) are the alien's aura, not elements.
- "Mini bosses" from Q1 are read as the chapter bosses. Glowing-aura monsters near them are
  read as this story's version of elites; that is a design call for the orchestrator, not made here.
- The king is found badly hurt but alive in every draft, because "who won?" needs an answer the
  player can see, and a dead king would need a scene the game has no system for.
- Every boss has one attack dodged by jumping and one dodged by changing lane, and a signature
  drop with its item kind named, because the bible asks for those (D22, D23, §6).

## Questions the winning draft will still need answered

1. Tone (Q2b) is still open in the World session. Drafts 2 and 5 are the funnier end, 1 and 4
   the earnest end, 3 in between. Picking a draft mostly answers this.
2. Whether Chapter 3 onward is ever built decides how much of the tail matters. Each draft is
   written so Chapters 1 and 2 stand on their own for Early Access.
3. The four heroes' names, looks and personalities (Q3). Draft 4 gives each knight a chapter of
   their own, which shapes that answer more than the others do.
4. The shopkeeper (Q6) is not placed in any draft. Any of them can take one recurring shopkeeper
   or one per region.
