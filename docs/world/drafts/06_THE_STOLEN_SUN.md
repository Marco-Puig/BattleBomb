# Candidate 6 — The Stolen Sun

**Pitch:** an alien is stealing the kingdom's warmth. Stakes in the ground suck light out of the
land and feed it into monsters. The knights follow the stolen light and the king's trail to the
source of it all, and then to a mountain forge where the alien means to turn that light into an
army.

**Tone:** the most straightforward heroic adventure. A warm, ridiculous kingdom becomes a place
worth fighting for. The king is a legend who treats a slime cleanup like a legendary war. The
alien commander is a cold operator.

**Working names:** the artifact is the Hearthsun. The alien is the Commander.

---

## The answers this draft gives

- **The artifact** is the Hearthsun: a golden stone in an old hearth, the source of the land's
  plentiful magic. It warms the roots beneath the kingdom. Its sanctuary lies above the mountain
  pass.
- **Why the aliens want it:** the Commander's stakes steal light piecemeal and feed it into
  monsters as a crude trial. With the whole Hearthsun, run through machinery salvaged from his
  ship at the old royal foundry, he can empower his army permanently and turn the whole kingdom
  into his supply of soldiers.
- **The king** followed the Commander to the sanctuary and held its inner door, his broken sword
  wedged in the mechanism. He is found badly wounded there, and lives. When the knights get him
  out, the Commander uses the opening to tear the Hearthsun free and run.
- **Where it ends:** the abandoned royal foundry, its chimneys glowing.
- **Final boss:** the Commander, who draws the Hearthsun's remaining power into himself at the
  central furnace.
- **Endless:** the stakes. The Commander planted more than anyone counted, and every one left in
  the ground keeps feeding light into whatever stands near it. The Knight Squad pulls stakes at
  the crash site forever.

---

## The arc at a glance

1. **Prelude** — village, celebration, crash, the king's reveal, minions. Outside: the ship, the
   glowing slimes, and the village in danger again.
2. **Chapter 1: the Village** — the stakes, the frozen millpond, the mill slime. The wagons of
   stolen light leave for the forest with the king's sword-cuts in their armour.
3. **Chapter 2: the Bright Forest** — follow the wagons. Boss. The king's trail turns up.
4. **Chapter 3: the Abbey and the Pass** — the mural, the broken blade, the sanctuary. The king.
   The Commander takes the Hearthsun down the ore railway.
5. **Chapter 4: the Foundry** — a furnace full of stolen summer. The Commander.
6. **After** — the light comes home. Endless.

## Prelude

As written in `../PRELUDE_NOTES.md`. This draft's specifics:

- **The celebration:** the king treats the slime cleanup like the end of a legendary war. A
  servant struggles past carrying a commemorative painting that is already finished.
- **The crashed ship:** an alien search device projects the image of a golden stone sitting in
  a hearth. Speeding through: they came for a glowing rock.
- **Outside:** the king and the Commander are gone. The slimes are swollen with unnatural light.
  The village is in danger again, and whatever happened to the king, the knights still have a job.

## Chapter 1 — The Village

**Region:** the tutorial village and its farmland. **Climate:** neutral: the teaching chapter.

**What you see on arrival:** alien stakes driven into the ground, glowing threads being sucked up
out of the earth and fed into the monsters standing near them. Beyond the stakes, flowers droop
and a millpond has frozen over. A baker is trying to thaw dough by sitting on it.

**The road:** the land's ordinary creatures: slimes as grunts, thrown-turnip scarecrows as
ranged, will-o'-wisps as casters, a boar as brute. Plain versions first; stake-fed glowing ones
nearer the mill.

**The mid-chapter beat:** the knights break a stake. The threads snap back into the ground and
the flowers around it stand up. Speeding through: the damage can be undone.

**The boss:** the Mill Slime, enormous, sitting on the mill and fed by the biggest stake in the
village. It bounces along the lane and lands (jump the splash) and rolls across one depth (change
lane). **Drop:** a pair of boots, *Millstompers*.

**Handoff:** the Mill Slime bursts and the local apparatus breaks with it. The pond melts and the
mill turns again. On the road out, the departing alien force's wagons are carrying jars of
stolen light toward the forest, and their armour carries unmistakable cuts from a golden sword.
Follow the wagons: they lead to the trouble and to the king.

---

## Chapter 2 — The Bright Forest

**Region:** a vivid forest, logging tracks, the wagon road. **Climate:** Earth strengthened, Air
weakened.

**What you see on arrival:** the wagon ruts, and beside them more stakes, and beside the stakes
the forest's creatures swelling and glowing. Where a stake has been pulled, the wood is bright
again.

**The road:** forest creatures: beetle-folk as grunts, acorn-throwing squirrel-folk as ranged,
moss shamans as casters, a walking stump as brute. Ordinary first; glowing near the wagon camps.

**The mid-chapter beat:** a smashed barricade, and beyond it a group of rescued villagers each
wearing a piece of the king's enormous ceremonial cape. Speeding through: the king was here, and
he was winning.

**The boss:** the Wagon Master, a forest brute the size of a barn that pulls the biggest wagon of
stolen light and is fed from it. It charges the lane with the wagon behind it (jump its lowered
head) and swings the wagon's chain across one depth (change lane). **Drop:** a bow, *Lightline*.

**Handoff:** the Wagon Master falls and the wagon's jars break, sending ribbons of light back
into the trees. The other wagons are already on the road up toward a flooded abbey, and beyond it
a mountain pass. On the road: a broken section of golden blade. He was winning fights, but not
the pursuit.

---

## Chapter 3 — The Abbey and the Pass

**Region:** flooded abbey grounds, then the mountain pass, then the sanctuary above it.
**Climate:** Ice strengthened, Fire weakened: the cold spreading from the stolen warmth.

**What you see on arrival:** the abbey half underwater, its orchard frozen, its checkpoint room
holding a simple mural: a golden stone warming the roots beneath the kingdom, and a sanctuary
above a mountain pass. Speeding through: the glowing rock lives up there.

**The road:** abbey and mountain creatures: frog-folk as grunts, spear-throwing yeti-folk as
ranged, frost sprites as casters, a stone ram as brute. The Commander's own soldiers from the
prelude join in on the pass.

**The mid-chapter beat:** the king. In the ruined sanctuary, braced against the Hearthsun's inner
door, badly wounded, his broken sword wedged into the mechanism. He followed the Commander here
and stopped him carrying the stone away. He cannot survive another attack. Speeding through: the
king held the door and is hurt.

**The boss:** the Commander's Guard, the biggest alien brute of the prelude's kind, left to finish
the job. It slams the sanctuary floor along the lane (jump the shockwave) and sweeps a glowing
halberd across one depth (change lane). **Drop:** a chestpiece, *Sanctuary Plate*.

**Handoff:** the knights get the king out. The Commander uses the opening: he tears the Hearthsun
from its cradle and flees along an old ore railway, in plain sight. As the stone leaves, light
drains from the valley below. The wounded king can only watch. The rails run down to a foundry
whose chimneys are already glowing.

---

## Chapter 4 — The Foundry

**Region:** the abandoned royal foundry, fitted with machinery salvaged from the ship: ore yards,
furnace galleries, the central furnace. **Climate:** Fire strengthened, Ice weakened.

**The road:** the Commander's newly armoured beasts, the strongest forms of every family met on
the journey, so the whole trip's combat lessons and gear pay off. Breaking each feed apparatus
releases ribbons of light back down the valley.

**The final boss:** the Commander, at the central furnace, drawing the Hearthsun's remaining power
into himself: the same alien who beat the king, now wielding the magic he stole from the places
the players saved. He stamps a ring of molten light across the floor (jump it) and vents a jet of
furnace-fire down one depth (change lane). **Drop:** a pet, *Hearth-Ember*, a small flame that
follows the player; the Hearthsun itself is story.

**The ending:** the Commander falls. The Hearthsun drops from the furnace and the player picks it
up with Light before the foundry consumes it.

---

## After

The results screen. Then the light goes home, in a short sequence through places the player
recognises: the frozen mill runs, the abbey orchard blooms, villagers reopen their doors. At the
homecoming feast the king, one arm in a sling, tries to repeat his robe-ripping entrance. A
servant quietly helps with the sleeves. The village presents its own commemorative painting: the
knights magnificent, the king tiny in the background and visibly delighted anyway.

**Endless:** the Commander planted more stakes than anyone counted. Every one still in the ground
keeps feeding light into whatever stands near it, so scattered empowered monsters remain. The
Knight Squad pulls stakes at the crash site, forever. That is Endless mode.

---

## Speeding through

Village, castle, crash, buff king, fight outside. Something is sucking the light out of the ground
and putting it into monsters. Break the stakes and the light comes back. Follow the wagons of
light. Follow the king's cape and his broken sword. Find the glowing rock and the king holding
the door. The alien takes the rock down the rails to a forge. Beat him there. Bring the light home.
